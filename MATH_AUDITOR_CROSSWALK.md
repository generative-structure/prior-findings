# MATH_AUDITOR_CROSSWALK.md

Every equation and formal result in the main text of `prior-findings.tex`,
with its symbols, an auditor's reading, a numerical example, and why it
matters operationally. Appendix propositions are listed at the end.

Symbols used throughout: `F_t` recorded finding this year; `C_t` underlying
condition this year; `f` prior-period record (1 after a finding year, 0 after
a clean year); `q_f` observed finding probability; `r_f` underlying-condition
probability; `d_f` detection probability given the condition exists;
`R_obs = q1/q0`; `R_lat = r1/r0`; `M = d1/d0`. Two symbols beyond these are
justified where introduced: `M̄`, the largest M the organization is prepared to
defend (Section 3), and `s`, the sensitivity of a reference procedure
(Section 8). `M` is target-specific: it is defined for a stated condition,
finding class or procedure, and different targets can have different `d0`,
`d1` and `M` (Section 8).

## Appendix propositions

| Proposition | Statement | Audit reading |
|---|---|---|
| A.1 Two-factor identity | `q_f = r_f d_f`; `r_f ≥ q_f`; `R_obs = R_lat M` | The recorded rate is the problem rate times the catch rate; the recorded ratio is the underlying ratio times the follow-up multiplier. |
| A.2 Unrestricted bounds | `R_lat ∈ [q1, 1/q0]`, sharp | The record says how common the problem must be in each group but not how much more common in one group than the other. |
| A.3 Monotone detection | `d1 ≥ d0 ⇒ R_lat ∈ [q1, R_obs]`; `R_obs < 1 ⇒ R_lat < 1`; `M ≤ M̄ ⇒ R_lat ≥ R_obs/M̄` | If follow-up only raises the catch rate, the recorded ratio is a ceiling and a defended ceiling on M gives a floor. |
| A.4 Remediation reversal | `r1 < r0`: `q1 > q0 ⇔ d1/d0 > r0/r1` | Recorded findings rise after remediation whenever follow-up raises the catch rate by more than the problem fell. |
| A.5 Full-history nonidentification | any `r(h) ∈ [q(h), 1]` with `d(h) = q(h)/r(h)` reproduces the whole history law | More years alone do not settle which story is true; restricted models can, and identification comes from the restriction. |
| A.6 Reference observation under a common measurement rule | representative within each history group; common `s` cancels; `M = R_obs/R_lat` | A reference sample representing each history group, worked under the same rule with common sensitivity, reveals the underlying ratio and hence M. |
| A.7 Two conditionally independent procedures | with `a`, `b` constant across the pooled stratum: `a = P_AB/P_B`, `b = P_AB/P_A`, `r = P_A P_B / P_AB` | Overlap between two independent procedures reveals each one's catch rate and the problems both missed. |
| A.8 Observation-only dynamics | i.i.d. condition plus follow-up rule ⇒ Markov record with `ρ_1 ≤ p` | A follow-up rule alone makes a memoryless problem look persistent in the record. |
